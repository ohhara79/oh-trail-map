# The 2D map as a minimap in Walk and playback

## Context

In 3D Walk and playback the camera sits at eye height, and bare SRTM terrain gives
little to go by: `#attitude3d` says which way you face, but not where you are. The
user wanted the 2D map on screen alongside, on phones as well.

The 2D map was already there to use. In 3D, `#map` is only `visibility: hidden`
(`src/style.css`, the map-area block), so the Leaflet map stays alive and in sync:
trails, selection dimming, the halo, national points, the GPS marker and the
profile's cursor dot. Showing it small costs no second map, no duplicated layer
code, and no cold tile cache. `createMap`'s `ResizeObserver` already handles a
change of container size.

Two choices were the user's: **north-up**, with an arrow at the centre turning
rather than the map (Leaflet cannot rotate, and labels stay upright); and the
minimap **hides with the rest** of the chrome on a tap during playback.

## Space on a phone

The top-left corner, below `#expand`, is the one place nothing in 3D reaches. The
bars and the joystick are at the bottom, the button column is on the right, and
`#mode3d` is at the top centre.

| Screen | Minimap | Spans (from top) | Nearest below |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phone portrait 390×~750 | 120px (200 enlarged) | 56–176 (256) | profile top ≈ 614 |
| Phone landscape 844×~360 | 100px (150 enlarged) | 56–156 (206) | joystick top 216, profile top 224 |
| Desktop | 180px (280 enlarged) | 56–236 (336); 12– with the panel open | — |

## Change

1. **`src/style.css`: the inset.**
   `#app[data-view='3d']:is([data-mode3d='walk'], [data-mode3d='playback']) #map`
   is shown at `--minimap-top` / `--minimap-left`, sized by `--minimap`, with
   `z-index: 950` (over `#map3d`, under the `.hud-bar`s and the drawer) and the
   `.hud-bar` border. Its controls, popups and tooltips are hidden, and its panes
   take no pointer events, so a click lands on the map itself. Orbit keeps the old
   rule and shows no minimap.
2. **`#minimap-you` (`index.html`, `src/style.css`)**: a dark arrow with a white
   edge at the inset's centre, turned by `--yaw`. It is deliberately not the blue
   GPS dot, which also shows on the minimap where you physically are.
3. **`src/view3d.ts`: `onCamera`**, fired from `reportCamera` with the eye's
   lat/lon/yaw and eye height, deduplicated like `hud.setAttitude`. It is called
   from the `FirstPerson` frame callback, and also as Walk and `walkTrail` begin,
   so the minimap is on the spot during the flight down.
4. **`src/main.ts`: `setMinimap` and `followCamera`.** Opening 3D disables Leaflet's
   gestures, and `close3d` enables them again (it already `setView`s 2D back).
   `followCamera` turns the arrow, picks z15/14/13 for eye 1.7/20/80m, and skips
   `setView` when the eye moved under a pixel. A `resize` listener re-centres, since
   the `ResizeObserver`'s `invalidateSize({ pan: false })` keeps the corner rather
   than the centre, and a still eye reports nothing new. The map click handler,
   reachable in 3D only as the minimap, toggles `data-minimap='large'`.
5. **Tap-to-hide**: `#map` and `#minimap-you` join `CHROME` in `src/hud3d.ts` and the
   three `data-chrome` / reduced-motion lists in `src/style.css`. Because the inset
   rule outranks the shared `data-chrome` rule, a heavier rule restates the
   `visibility: hidden`.

## Verification

1. `npm run build` passes.
2. Headless Chrome against `npm run preview`, at 390×844 and 844×390 (mobile,
   touch) and 1280×800. The inset sits at 12,56 at 120 / 100 / 180px, with the
   arrow at its centre. It clears the joystick, `#mode3d`, the right-hand column,
   the playback bar and the profile.
3. Playback on the phone, then a click on the inset: it grows to 200px and still
   clears everything. Setting `data-chrome='hidden'` hides it and the arrow with the
   bar (checked under reduced motion, since headless Chrome does not advance the
   fade).
4. By hand: walking moves the map under the arrow, and looking around turns only
   the arrow. Cycling eye height zooms out. Orbit hides the minimap. Closing 3D
   gives back a full-size, draggable 2D map at the walked-to place.
